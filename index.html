Completed project: how the brain responds to sounds and pictures

A full EEG analysis in Python with MNE: load a real recording, clean it, cut it into short windows around each sound and picture, average the windows, and test whether the two responses differ.

  • The sound response peaks near 97 ms. The picture response peaks later, near 187 ms.
  • The scalp pattern flips between the two: sounds are negative over the upper-central scalp, pictures are positive toward the front.
  • A test that shuffles the labels 5000 times found the difference in the 150 to 220 ms window unlikely to be chance (p < 0.001).

One person, 20 seconds, 11 windows per condition, and the analysis window was chosen after looking at the data, so the p-value is optimistic. The write-up lists every limit.

Current project: can a quantum state survive in a warm brain?

Some researchers suggest that quantum effects inside neurons help produce consciousness. Others say the brain is too warm and wet for a quantum state to last long enough to matter. I'm testing that question with a simulation on an ordinary computer: one quantum bit, a warm environment around it, and a measurement of how fast it loses its quantum behavior, compared with how fast neurons work.

In progress. There are no results yet. Quantum consciousness is not established science.
